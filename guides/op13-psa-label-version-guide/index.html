OP-13 Carrying On His Will: identify the version from the PSA label

How to tell which OP-13 version is in a PSA slab: line 1, the #number and the variety line, with the old-number SP cards that share a label number.

By OPChase Research · Published 2026-10-28 · 6 min read

Short answer. An OP-13 PSA label tells you the version in three places: line 1 (the year and the OP13 set line), the #number (digits only) and the variety line. OP-13 has more special versions than most sets: the three SECs (#118 Luffy, #119 Ace, #120 Sabo) each have five, five Five Elders cards have three, and nine SP and Treasure Rare cards from older sets keep their old numbers. Every variety wording below comes from our PSA spec data, not from a PSA cert page we can cite, so each one is marked unconfirmed.

What the OP-13 set line looks like

  • Our PSA spec data files OP-13 English cards as "2025 One Piece OP13 Carrying on His Will" (unconfirmed on a cert page for this guide).
  • The label prints the number as digits only, for example "#118". The prefix (OP13-, OP09-, EB02-…) is printed on the card, bottom right. Read it through the slab.
  • Bandai's official English list for OP-13 has 175 entries, including SP cards and one Treasure Rare that keep codes from older sets card list.

Store names such as "Super Alternate Art" are catalog names, not PSA wording. On OP-13, the store's "Super Alternate Art" is the non-red manga card, and "Red Super Alternate Art" is the red one. Bandai's product page describes the red version as "A new Red Super Alt-Art only for this booster".

Manga vs Red Manga

  • Both are SEC parallels of #118, #119 and #120. On the label, the only difference we expect is the word RED at the start of the variety line unconfirmed.
  • A title that says only "manga" or only "super" does not tell you which one it is. Read the variety line to the end.
  • Our card pages list them as two versions: OP13-118, OP13-119, OP13-120.

OP13-119 Ace Wanted and the errata

Bandai's official card list marks the OP13-119 SP CARD (Wanted) entry as "Errata Card", and Bandai published a notice about its [On Play] text notice. Some #119 Ace Wanted slabs carry WANTED ALT ART-ERRATA instead of WANTED ALTERNATE ART (unconfirmed in this guide). How to tell the misprinted card from the corrected one is in our guide OP13-119 Ace Wanted: misprint or corrected.

How to check any OP-13 slab

  1. Line 1: the year and the OP13 set line. EN at the end means an English card.
  2. Number: digits only. Match it with the code on the card, bottom right.
  3. Name: #118, #119, #004, #028, #009, #030, #058 and #111 each belong to more than one character in OP-13. Match the name and the number together.
  4. Variety line: no variety, ALTERNATE ART, SPECIAL ALTERNATE ART, MANGA ALTERNATE ART, RED MANGA ALTERNATE ART and WANTED ALTERNATE ART are separate PSA entries. Read every word.
  5. Cert lookup: type the cert number into psacard.com/cert/ and check that year, set, name, number and variety match the slab.

Method and limits

  • This is not an authenticity or grading guide. A matching label does not prove a slab is genuine.
  • It says nothing about values or grading counts.
  • No PSA cert page was read for this guide; every variety wording is marked unconfirmed until one is cited.

Where the facts come from

  • Card numbers, names, rarities, entry counts: Bandai official English card list, OP-13 Carrying On His Will (en.onepiece-cardgame.com/cardlist/?series=569113), 175 entries, as held in the OPChase card database (copy of 2026-10-22).
  • OP13-119 errata: Bandai notice (en.onepiece-cardgame.com/topics/notice_op13-119.php).
  • Variety wording: PSA spec names as held in the OPChase card database. These are not cert pages, so every wording is marked unconfirmed.
  • Version names used by stores: the TCGplayer catalog as held in the OPChase card database. Store names are not PSA wording.
