Manga Rare vs Alternate Art vs SP vs Red Manga: One Piece TCG rarities explained for collectors

Most One Piece chase cards are versions of the same card number. What makes one worth a few hundred dollars and another tens of thousands is the version, and the version is written on the PSA label.

Published 2026-10-01 · Prices: OPChase Index, data as of 2026-09-30 · 7 min read

Quick answer. Alternate Art is the common chase parallel. Manga Rare is the scarce manga-style parallel and usually the most valuable version of a card in its set. SP is an older card brought back in a special frame in a later set. Red Manga is the OP-13 anniversary tier above Manga. On a PSA slab, the variety line tells you which one you have.

In the One Piece Card Game the printed rarity (C, UC, R, SR, SEC, L for Leader, P for Promo) tells you about the card in a deck. Collectors care about something else: the version. The same card number can come as a regular print, an alternate art, a manga rare, an SP in a later set, a promo, and more. PSA files each version as its own spec, so each has its own population and its own price.

All prices below are PSA 10 values from the OPChase Index (data as of 2026-09-30), so you can compare versions of the same card.

Alternate Art (AA)

Alternate arts are the "parallel" foils that show up about once per box in English. They are the entry point to chase collecting. Because they are common, PSA 10 populations are high: OP05-119 Luffy AA has a PSA 10 pop of 5,061 (GemRate, 2026-09-28). On a PSA label the variety reads ALTERNATE ART.

Manga Rare

Manga rares (Bandai's "comic parallel") put the character on manga-panel artwork. They are much scarcer than alt arts and sit at the top of most sets' value charts. OP05 Gear 5 Luffy shows the gap: Manga $17,453 vs Alternate Art $472, with PSA 10 pops of 1,169 vs 5,061. On a PSA label the variety reads MANGA ALTERNATE ART. Watch for reprints: PRB01 reprinted some manga rares under its own set line. See the OP05-119 label guide.

SP (Special card)

An SP brings an older card back in a later set with a special frame. The tricky part: PSA keeps the original number but uses the new set's name. OP09-119 Luffy's SP from OP-13 shows an OP13 set line, #119 and the variety SPECIAL ALTERNATE ART. Search only "OP09-119" and you will miss it; search "OP13 #119" and you might think it is an OP-13 card. Its PSA 10 is $654, against $4,566 for the OP09 Manga of the same card.

Anniversary SPs are their own family. OP05-119 Luffy's 3rd Anniversary SP from OP-11 comes in Gold and Silver, labelled "3RD ANNIVERSARY-GOLD" and "-SILVER". They are separate specs; the Gold PSA 10 is $17,027.

Red Manga (Red Super Alt Art)

OP-13, Carrying On His Will, was the third-anniversary set. It added a tier above the regular manga: red-foil "Red Super" alt arts, which collectors call Red Manga. GemRate lists the spec as RED MANGA ALTERNATE ART; the non-red versions of the same cards are filed under MANGA ALTERNATE ART. For OP13-118 Luffy, the Red is $30,178 and the non-red Manga is $3,705. OP-13 was reprinted in 2026, which adds copies of its regular pulls; the OP-13 set page lists every version.

Other names you will see

  • Wanted (poster): wanted-poster art inserted into a later set (ST01-012 in OP-03, OP05-119 in OP-09). PSA: "WANTED ALTERNATE ART".
  • TR (Treasure Rare): textured premium cards in English sets OP-06 to OP-15. PSA: "TREASURE RARE".
  • Serialized: numbered prize cards from events. Very thin markets.
  • Promos (P-xxx): the promo number names the card, not the version. One number like P-001 has many versions (pre-release, winner, store championship), each its own spec.

How to use this when buying

  1. Decide which version you want, then search with the PSA variety wording, not only the card number.
  2. On every listing, read the label photo: set line, variety, number, and no "JAPANESE" for an English card.
  3. Compare the price with recent sales of the same spec. Each OPChase card page shows the sales behind its index value.
