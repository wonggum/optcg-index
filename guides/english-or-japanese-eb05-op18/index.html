English or Japanese? Telling the two editions apart before EB-05 and OP-18

EB-05 and OP-18 come out in English and Japanese about a day apart, with the same card numbers in both. Here is what Bandai's own English and Japanese pages show, so you can tell which edition a card, a slab or a listing is describing.

By OPChase Research · Published 2026-10-29 · 6 min read

Short answer. The card number does not tell you the language. Bandai's English and Japanese card lists both show EB-05 as EB05-001 to EB05-061 (card lists), and OP-18 is listed with 127+1 types on both product pages (OP-18). What differs is the language of the card name and text, the set title and the release date. Check those, not the number alone.

The two release calendars

Bandai runs separate English and Japanese sites, and each lists its own release date. For the two sets in this guide, the English date comes one day before the Japanese one:

What this means for a buyer: a listing for an EB-05 card dated before the English release date can only refer to a pre-release or a different edition, and you should read it carefully. The calendar alone never proves anything about a specific card, but it is a useful first question.

Same number, two languages

Both of Bandai's EB-05 card lists cover the same 61 card numbers, EB05-001 to EB05-061 (card lists). The number, rarity and stats line up; the name and text are in the site's language. One example from the two lists:

So if a slab, a photo or a listing title only gives "EB05-033", it has not told you the language. The name and the card text are what change. A card name written in Japanese script points to the Japanese edition; a name in English points to an English-language edition.

The set titles work the same way. The English OP-18 page calls the set "THE DOMINANCE OF GOD"; the Japanese page calls it 神の支配 (OP-18). Both use the code OP-18. If a listing quotes the English title, it is describing the English edition; if it quotes 神の支配, it is quoting the Japanese one.

English is not one region

Bandai's site language selector offers two English versions: English (Asia) and English (NA/EU/OC/LATAM/ME) (product lists). The English release dates in this guide come from the NA/EU/OC/LATAM/ME site. If you buy from the Asia region, check that region's Bandai page for its own dates; this guide does not cover it.

One EB-05 card where the English edition may vary

On October 23, 2026, Bandai's English site posted an erratum for EB-05 (erratum notice). In the card text of EB05-033 Nico Robin, the name "O-Robi" was printed where "Nico Robin" was intended. Bandai says the corrected wording is to be treated as correct, that the same applies to the alt-art card, and that "corrected cards are not available in some regions."

For a buyer, that means two English-language copies of EB05-033 may not have the same text, depending on where they were printed. The Japanese card list shows the name as ニコ・ロビン (card lists). The notice is on the English site; it does not say whether the Japanese printing is affected, so this guide makes no claim about it.

Contents per pack and rarity counts

The two product pages also describe EB-05 slightly differently, which helps if you are reading a box or a sealed listing (EB-05):

  • English page: "75+2 types in total"; rarities listed as Commons, Rares, Super Rares, Secret Rares, Leaders, Special Cards and DON!! Cards; tournament legal from the pre-release date in your region.
  • Japanese page: 6 cards per pack, 75+2 types; Leader 7, Common 29, Rare 21, Super Rare 9, Secret Rare 1, Special Card 8, DON!! 2.

For OP-18, the English page says "127+1 types in total" and that cards are tournament legal starting 7 days after the release date in your region; the Japanese page says 6 cards per pack, 127+1 types (OP-18).

A quick checklist before you buy

  1. Read the name, not only the number. EB05-xxx and OP18-xxx are shared by both editions.
  2. Match the set title. "THE DOMINANCE OF GOD" is the English OP-18 title; 神の支配 is the Japanese one.
  3. Check the date. English EB-05 is dated October 30, 2026 and English OP-18 November 20, 2026 on Bandai's English site.
  4. Ask which region. Bandai has two English sites, Asia and NA/EU/OC/LATAM/ME.
  5. For EB05-033, look at the card text. Bandai says the corrected text is not available in some regions.
  6. When in doubt, ask the seller for a clear photo of the card name and text.

Method and limits

  • Official: all dates, titles, counts, card fields and the erratum come from Bandai's English and Japanese sites, viewed 2026-10-29.
  • Not covered: how PSA prints the language on its labels. PSA's label pages could not be read for this guide, so we have left that out rather than guess. See our PSA label guide for what PSA's pages say about label lines in general.
  • What this guide is not: it is not a way to tell whether a card or slab is genuine, and it covers no grading counts or market values.
